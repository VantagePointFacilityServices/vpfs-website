Regular cleaning of shared surfaces helps limit the spread of some illnesses, but it is only one measure among several, and the dramatic percentage reductions in sick leave quoted in supplier marketing are not a safe basis for a business case. The reliable position is that cleaning supports a healthy workplace alongside hand hygiene, ventilation and staying home when unwell.

## What do health authorities say?

The Queensland Government's page on [influenza](https://www.qld.gov.au/health/condition/infections-and-parasites/viral-infections/influenza-the-flu) explains that flu spreads through droplets and tiny particles from an infected person, and that you can also catch it by touching contaminated surfaces and then touching your face. It recommends cleaning frequently touched surfaces such as door handles, washing hands regularly with soap and water or an alcohol-based hand rub, and staying home until you feel better and have no fever. Cleaning is therefore part of the advice, not the whole of it.

## Why be cautious with statistics?

Searches for "cleaning and sick days" turn up many confident figures, such as large percentage reductions in absenteeism. Most come from suppliers of cleaning or hygiene services, often without a traceable study behind them. We do not quote them here, and we suggest you do not put them in a board paper unless you can trace them to a published study and check what it actually measured. A claim that cannot be traced is a weak foundation for a budget.

## What can you reasonably expect?

- **Fewer surfaces carrying germs:** regular cleaning of high-touch surfaces and washrooms reduces opportunities for transmission.
- **Visible care:** staff see that the workplace is looked after, which affects morale and trust, although this is harder to measure.
- **Fewer complaints and problems:** consistent cleaning prevents the build-up that leads to complaints about kitchens and washrooms.

## What else matters?

Sick leave depends on many things: workload, culture, vaccination, ventilation and whether people feel able to stay home when ill. A cleaning contract will not fix those. Treat it as one control in a wider approach and judge it by whether the tasks that matter are done, to standard, every time.

## How should you use this in a business case?

Argue from the specific: which surfaces and rooms are cleaned, how often and how you will verify it. Pair that with the guidance from health authorities, and avoid promising a number.
