High-touch surfaces are the things many people touch every day, such as door handles, light switches, lift buttons, taps and shared equipment. Name them in your cleaning scope and clean them more often than floors. Queensland Government advice on flu includes cleaning frequently touched surfaces such as door handles, and a scope that lists them by name makes sure it happens.

## What counts as a high-touch surface?

Think about where hands go. In most commercial buildings the list includes:

- Door handles, push plates and entry intercoms.
- Light switches and thermostats.
- Lift buttons and handrails.
- Taps, flush buttons and washroom dispensers.
- Kitchen handles: kettle, microwave, fridge and dishwasher.
- Meeting-room tables, chair arms and remotes.
- Shared equipment such as printers, photocopiers and phones.
- Reception counters and shared pens or tablets.

## How often should they be cleaned?

Frequency should follow use. A working guide:

| Setting | Suggested frequency |
|---|---|
| Busy shared areas such as entries, lifts and washrooms | Every cleaning visit, with a mid-day check in busy sites |
| Offices and meeting rooms | Every visit, or at least several times a week |
| Low-traffic areas | Weekly |
| During outbreaks or flu season | Increase as agreed with your cleaner |

This is a starting point for discussion, not a standard. Agree frequencies that suit your building and revisit them when conditions change. See [cleaning during flu season](/resources/healthy-workplace/air-and-environment/cleaning-during-flu-season/).

## Cleaning or disinfecting?

For most offices, regular cleaning with detergent is the baseline, and the Queensland Government notes that flu viruses can be removed by this kind of cleaning. Disinfectants have a place in clinical areas, after an outbreak or where your own risk assessment says so, and need the right product and contact time. Ask your cleaner which they use and why. See [when a commercial site needs a disinfectant](/resources/by-building-type/medical-and-dental/infection-control-expectations-in-clinics/) for the clinic case.

## How do you write it into a scope?

List the surfaces in each area, state the frequency and say what is used. For example: "Reception: wipe counter, door handles and intercom, every visit." Then ask for a checklist that records it, with photos of hotspots. See [how to write a cleaning scope of work](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/write-a-cleaning-scope-of-work/).

## What about staff?

Staff can help with a quick wipe of shared equipment such as keyboards, phones and desks, using wipes you supply. Cleaners do not usually clean personal electronics unless the scope says so.
