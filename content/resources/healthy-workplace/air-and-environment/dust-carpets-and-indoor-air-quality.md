Routine cleaning controls the dust that builds up on floors, ledges and carpets, and good vacuuming and dusting help keep an office comfortable. But indoor air quality depends on several things, and cleaning is only one of them. If staff report stuffiness, odours or symptoms, ask for a proper look at ventilation and the building, not just more cleaning.

## What affects indoor air quality?

Guidance for workplaces, such as Comcare's [health and safety representative helper on indoor air quality](https://www.comcare.gov.au/sites/default/files/docs/hsr-helper-indoor-air-quality.pdf), treats air quality as a combination of factors: sources of contaminants in the building, how well the ventilation system is designed and maintained, and how the building is used. Cleaning materials and activities are among the possible sources, which is a reason to choose and use products carefully.

## What can cleaning do?

- **Remove dust:** regular dusting of surfaces and vacuuming of floors keeps settled dust from building up and being disturbed.
- **Maintain carpets:** carpet traps dust and dirt, so vacuuming with a suitable machine and periodic deep cleaning help.
- **Keep wet areas clean:** washrooms and kitchens are where odours and mould problems start.
- **Avoid adding to the problem:** use appropriate products and dosing, and avoid over-use and strong fragrances.

## How should you specify it?

| Task | Typical frequency |
|---|---|
| Vacuum carpets in high-traffic areas | Every visit |
| Vacuum all floors | Weekly |
| Dust accessible surfaces and ledges | Weekly |
| Dust vents, light fittings and high surfaces | Quarterly or as needed |
| Carpet deep clean or extraction | Periodically, depending on use |

These are examples to adapt. Ask your cleaner what equipment is used, since vacuums vary in how well they capture fine dust.

## What can cleaning not fix?

It will not repair a ventilation system, remove sources such as damp or building materials or solve complaints that need a technical assessment. If problems persist, involve your building manager or a competent adviser, and check what your workplace health and safety obligations require.

## What about complaints about cleaning products?

Fragrance and chemical odours can bother some people. Ask which products are used and read the safety data sheets. See [safety data sheets and chemical management](/resources/compliance-and-risk/what-a-compliant-contractor-holds/safety-data-sheets-and-chemical-management/).
