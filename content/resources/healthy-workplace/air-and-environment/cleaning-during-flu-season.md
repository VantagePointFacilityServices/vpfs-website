During flu season, adjust your cleaning scope in three ways: clean high-touch surfaces more often, make sure hand-washing supplies never run out and tell staff what is being done. Cleaning is one part of the Queensland Government's advice, which also stresses hand hygiene and staying home when unwell, so do not treat extra cleaning as a substitute for those.

## What does the health advice say?

The Queensland Government's page on [influenza](https://www.qld.gov.au/health/condition/infections-and-parasites/viral-infections/influenza-the-flu) says flu spreads through droplets and tiny particles when an infected person coughs, sneezes, talks or breathes, and can also be caught by touching contaminated surfaces and then touching your face. It recommends cleaning frequently touched surfaces, washing hands regularly and staying home until you feel better and have no fever.

## What can you adjust in the scope?

- **Frequency of high-touch cleaning:** increase it in busy areas such as entries, lifts, washrooms and shared equipment, or add a mid-day pass on busy sites. See [high-touch surfaces: what to clean and how often](/resources/healthy-workplace/for-staff/high-touch-surfaces-what-to-clean-and-how-often/).
- **Supplies:** check soap, paper towel and hand rub stock every visit and top up before they run out.
- **Washrooms and kitchens:** make sure the basics are done to standard, since these are shared and heavily used.
- **Waste:** empty bins promptly, including tissue bins.

## How should you decide how much extra?

Increase for as long as it helps and no longer. Agree the extra work in writing with a price and an end date, so a seasonal change does not become a permanent cost or a dispute. Our guide to [what affects a cleaning quote](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/what-affects-a-cleaning-quote/) explains why frequency changes the price.

## What should you tell staff?

Let staff know what has been increased and what they can do: wash hands, use the supplies provided and stay home when unwell. Encourage them to report empty dispensers and problems. A short note on the kitchen and washroom doors works well.

## What about outbreaks?

If there is a confirmed outbreak at your site, follow the advice from health authorities and your own procedures, and talk to your cleaner about whether a different product or approach is needed. For clinical settings, see [infection-control expectations in clinics](/resources/by-building-type/medical-and-dental/infection-control-expectations-in-clinics/).

## What should you avoid promising?

Do not tell staff or customers that extra cleaning will prevent illness. It helps limit spread, but it is one control among several. See [does a clean office reduce sick days?](/resources/healthy-workplace/for-staff/does-a-clean-office-reduce-sick-days/).
