Customers, clients, candidates and visitors judge your business in the first minute, and they do it on what they can see, smell and touch. They will not read your cleaning scope. They will notice the reception desk, the entry glass, the lift and the washroom, and they will draw conclusions about how you run everything else.

That makes the visitor-facing parts of the building the place to be most deliberate about cleaning. The standard does not need to be expensive, but it does need to be consistent, because one bad day in a washroom can undo a lot of good work.

## The guides in this cluster

- **First impressions: reception and washrooms** covers what visitors notice and how to specify it.
- **Washroom standards and complaints** shows how to set a washroom standard, prevent complaints and respond when they arrive.
