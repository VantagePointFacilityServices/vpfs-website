Set a washroom standard in writing, such as which tasks are done, how often and what "clean" looks like, make sure supplies never run out, and check regularly. Most washroom complaints come from the same few causes: stock-outs, smells, wet floors and missed visits, and all can be prevented by a clear scope and routine checks.

## Why is the washroom the main complaint?

Everyone uses it, nobody wants to see it neglected and problems are obvious. Workplaces are required to provide amenities that are clean, safe and maintained. Queensland's [code of practice on managing the work environment and facilities](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/qld/managing-the-work-environment-and-facilities-code-of-practice-2021/42501) covers toilets and washing facilities for workers, so a neglected washroom is also a compliance concern, not just an annoyance.

## What should the standard include?

- **Fixtures:** toilets, urinals and basins cleaned and sanitised, with no marks or build-up.
- **Surfaces:** mirrors, benches, partitions and dispensers wiped.
- **Floors:** swept and mopped, dry and non-slip when users arrive.
- **Supplies:** toilet paper, soap and paper towel stocked, with a note on who orders and when.
- **Waste:** bins emptied before they overflow, including sanitary bins where provided.
- **Odour:** no lingering smell, which usually means cleaning the cause, not masking it.

## How often?

Frequency follows use. A busy site may need a mid-day check as well as an evening clean. Agree the frequency per washroom and review it when headcount changes. See [what affects a cleaning quote](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/what-affects-a-cleaning-quote/) for why fixture count and use drive the cleaning time.

## How do you prevent complaints?

Give staff and visitors a way to report problems fast, such as a number or a QR code on the door, and pass them to the cleaner the same day. Keep a stock of supplies on site. Inspect regularly, with photos.

## How should you respond to a complaint?

Fix the problem straight away, tell the person it has been dealt with, record it and look for the cause. If the same problem repeats, treat it as a pattern and raise it under the contract.
