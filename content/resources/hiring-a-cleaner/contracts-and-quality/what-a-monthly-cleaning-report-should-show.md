A useful monthly cleaning report shows how the site performed against the agreed scope, which issues were found and how they were fixed, supporting photos, any recurring patterns and what is planned for next month. If a report is mostly reassurance and has no specifics, it is not telling you anything.

## What should be in it?

### Results against the scope

The report should follow your scope area by area: reception, offices, kitchens, washrooms and so on. For each, it should say whether tasks were completed to standard. This is the section you read first, because it connects the report directly to what you are paying for.

### Inspection findings

It should list what the supervisor found on inspection, including failures. A report with no failures ever recorded is a warning sign, because no site is perfect every day. What matters is that issues are found, logged and closed.

### Issues raised and how they were fixed

Include complaints from your own team, what was done about each and how long it took. This shows responsiveness, which is often more telling than the absence of problems.

### Photos

Dated before-and-after photos or photos of hotspot areas make findings concrete. They also let you check without being on site. Hotspots are the places that slip first, such as washrooms, kitchen benches and entry points.

### Patterns and trends

A single month is a snapshot. A good report notes repeat issues, such as the same area failing twice, and what is being done about the cause.

### Consumables, hazards and incidents

Where relevant, the report should note supplies used or low, any hazards found and any incidents on site, with the action taken.

### Next month

A short list of planned periodic tasks, such as carpet or glass work, and any changes needed to the scope.

## How should you use the report?

Read the results against the scope, check a few items against the photos, and look at the issues log. Then walk the site yourself occasionally to confirm the report matches what you see. Raise anything that does not, in writing. The audit process behind the report is explained in [how cleaning quality is audited](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/how-cleaning-quality-is-audited/).

## What should the contract say about reporting?

It should state that reports are provided, how often, in what form and that you can inspect the underlying records. See [cleaning contract terms to check](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/cleaning-contract-terms-to-check/).
