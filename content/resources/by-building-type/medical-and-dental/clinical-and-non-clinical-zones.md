To specify cleaning for a practice, divide it into zones by risk, write down which tasks, products and equipment apply to each zone and make sure the contractor follows that separation. Reception cleaning and treatment-room cleaning should never be the same job done with the same cloth.

## What are the zones?

Practices differ, so use your own infection-control plan to set the zones. A common way to think about it:

| Zone | Examples | Cleaning approach |
|---|---|---|
| Non-clinical | Reception, waiting room, offices, corridors | Routine cleaning, with attention to high-touch surfaces such as door handles and counters |
| Clinical | Treatment and consulting rooms, procedure areas | Cleaning to your written procedure, using specified products and separate equipment |
| Wet areas | Washrooms, sluice or utility rooms | Specific fixtures and equipment, kept separate from other zones |
| Staff areas | Kitchen, staff room | Food-area cleaning, separate from clinical equipment |

## Why separate equipment matters

Moving cloths, mops or buckets from a washroom or clinical area to a waiting room can spread contamination. Many practices require separate or colour-coded equipment for each zone. Whether you need it is a matter for your infection-control policy, and the point is to state it in the scope so the contractor does it every time.

## What should the scope say about each zone?

For each zone state the tasks, the frequency, the products to be used and any limits, such as "do not clean inside instrument cabinets" or "do not move patient equipment". Say which areas must be left alone because staff clean them as part of patient care.

## How should high-touch surfaces be handled?

Door handles, light switches, counters, chair arms and shared equipment handles are cleaned more often than floors in most practices. List them by name and frequency so they are not assumed.

## How do you check it is being done?

Ask for a visit checklist signed by the cleaner and a regular report against the scope, with photos. Walk the practice yourself occasionally. [How cleaning quality is audited](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/how-cleaning-quality-is-audited/) explains the process.
