In a medical or dental practice, your infection-control plan sets the standard, and the cleaning contractor's job is to carry out the cleaning parts of that plan consistently, using the products and methods you specify, and to show you evidence. The practice stays responsible for infection control. The cleaner supports it.

## Where do the standards come from?

National guidance for infection prevention in healthcare is published by the NHMRC, including the Australian Guidelines for the Prevention and Control of Infection in Healthcare, along with other guidance on the [NHMRC infection prevention page](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/health-advice/public-health/preventing-infection). Your profession's accrediting body and your own infection-control policy also apply. Use those documents, not a cleaning company's brochure, as the reference for what your practice requires.

## Who is responsible for what?

The practice is responsible for its infection-control policy, for clinical cleaning that is part of patient care, such as instrument reprocessing, and for telling the contractor what is needed. The contractor is responsible for environmental cleaning of the areas in the agreed scope, following the written procedures, and for using products and equipment appropriately.

## What should you require of a cleaning contractor?

- Written procedures for each zone that match your policy.
- Training for staff in your site's rules, including what they must not touch.
- Correct products, with safety data sheets, and correct dilution and contact times as specified.
- Separate or colour-coded equipment for different zones, where your policy calls for it.
- A way to report spills, sharps or other hazards they find and not handle them.
- A checklist or record for each visit and a regular report against the scope.

## What about waste?

Clinical waste is usually handled separately under the practice's own arrangements. Be explicit in the scope about who handles which waste streams, so cleaners are not expected to handle what they are not trained for.

## What should you ask a contractor?

Ask what experience they have in clinics, how staff are inducted, how they prevent cross-contamination between zones and how they would respond to an outbreak or a deep-clean request.
