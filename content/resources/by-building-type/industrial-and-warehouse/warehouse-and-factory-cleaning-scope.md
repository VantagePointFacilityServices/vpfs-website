A warehouse or factory cleaning scope should divide the site into zones, specify tasks and frequencies for each, set out the safety controls cleaners must follow and state clearly what operations staff clean themselves. The safety rules of the site decide what is practical, so write them into the scope.

## What zones does the site have?

Most industrial sites have an office or front-of-house area, an operational floor with racking or machinery, loading and dock areas, workshops or production areas and amenities. Each has different tasks and risks. List them and mark the ones cleaners may enter, the ones they may enter only in certain hours and the ones they must not.

## What tasks belong in the scope?

| Zone | Typical tasks |
|---|---|
| Offices and reception | Standard office tasks, bins, floors, glass |
| Operational floor | Sweeping, scrubbing of aisles and walkways, spill response only as agreed |
| Loading and dock areas | Sweeping, rubbish removal, drains kept clear |
| Workshops and production | Only if agreed, with site-specific procedures |
| Amenities | Full clean, restock |
| High level | Dust on ledges, light fittings and beams, usually periodic |

## What safety controls apply?

Industrial sites carry hazards cleaners should not be left to discover: moving vehicles, forklifts, machinery, chemicals, dust and work at height. The scope should say how cleaners are inducted, which areas need escorts, which hours to avoid, what personal protective equipment is required and how to report hazards. As the site operator you have duties to contractors working on your site. See the [WorkSafe Queensland guidance on managing the work environment and facilities](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/qld/managing-the-work-environment-and-facilities-code-of-practice-2021/42501) for the framework.

## What should be excluded?

State what the cleaning contract does not cover: industrial processes, hazardous or contaminated waste, machinery cleaning and anything requiring specialist licences. Be clear which spills are cleaned by operations and which by the contractor.

## Who supplies equipment?

Large floors may need machine scrubbing. Say whether the contractor brings equipment or uses yours, and who is responsible for charging, storage and damage.

## How should you check the work?

Use a checklist by zone and walk the site with the contractor's supervisor. See [how cleaning quality is audited](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/how-cleaning-quality-is-audited/).
