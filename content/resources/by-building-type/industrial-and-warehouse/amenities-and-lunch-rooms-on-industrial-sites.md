Industrial sites must provide workers with clean, safe and hygienic amenities, so the toilets, washing areas and lunch rooms need their own line in the cleaning scope, with frequencies that match how many people use them. They are the spaces your workforce notices most, and the ones inspectors and staff complaints tend to focus on.

## What does the framework expect?

Queensland's Managing the work environment and facilities Code of Practice 2021 covers facilities for workers, including toilets, drinking water, washing and dining areas, and expects them to be maintained in a clean, safe and accessible condition. Read the [code of practice](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/qld/managing-the-work-environment-and-facilities-code-of-practice-2021/42501) and your own safety advisor's requirements, and treat the cleaning scope as one part of meeting them.

## What should the scope cover?

- **Toilets and washrooms:** full clean of fixtures, floors and walls, restocking of paper, soap and sanitary supplies, and removal of waste.
- **Showers and change rooms**, where provided.
- **Lunch rooms and kitchenettes:** benches, tables, sinks, microwaves and fridge exteriors, bins and floors.
- **Hand-washing stations** near the floor, kept stocked and clear.
- **Drinking-water points**, wiped and checked.
- **Lockers and boot areas**, where agreed.

## How often should they be cleaned?

Frequency follows use, not floor area. A site with several shifts or a large headcount needs more visits, and a mid-shift check may be sensible for busy washrooms. Set the frequency per area and say who restocks and how stock is ordered.

## How do you handle shift work?

Industrial sites often run around the clock, so cleaning may need to happen while people are using the space. Agree cleaning times with shift breaks in mind, use wet-floor signs and plan how a washroom is closed and reopened.

## What should the contractor report?

Ask them to report broken fixtures, leaks, missing supplies and hazards found, to a named contact, straight away. A clean washroom with a broken tap is still a problem. Regular reporting is covered in [what a monthly cleaning report should show](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/what-a-monthly-cleaning-report-should-show/).
