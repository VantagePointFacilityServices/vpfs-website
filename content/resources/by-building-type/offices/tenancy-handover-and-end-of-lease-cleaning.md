For an office handover, read your lease to see exactly what it requires on exit, document the condition against the start-of-lease report, then scope the cleaning to match and book it before the inspection. Cleaning is only part of the obligation, and what counts as "clean" depends on the wording of your lease.

## What does your lease actually say?

Make good clauses vary a great deal. The Queensland Small Business Commissioner's [make good factsheet](https://www.qsbc.qld.gov.au/factsheets/make-good) lists leaving the premises clean and tidy as a common term, but notes that some leases require far more, such as removing fit-out or restoring walls. Read your lease before you plan anything, and get legal advice if it is unclear.

## What should you check against?

If a condition report was done at the start of the lease, use it as your baseline. Disputes commonly arise from vague clauses and a lack of evidence about the starting condition. If there is no report, photograph the tenancy now and keep the record.

## What should the handover scope include?

Handover cleaning is usually more thorough than routine cleaning. Typical items to specify include:

- Removal of rubbish and left-behind items, if the lease requires it.
- Washrooms and kitchens cleaned to a deeper level.
- Hard floors cleaned and, if the lease requires, carpets extracted.
- Internal glass, partitions and window frames.
- Skirting, doors and door frames, light switches and high-touch surfaces.
- Dusting at height, vents and light fittings.
- Marks removed from walls where this is cleaning rather than repainting.

Separate cleaning from repair and repainting, which are different trades and different costs.

## When should you book it?

Book the clean after the tenancy is emptied and any removalists and trades have finished, and before the landlord or agent inspects. Allow time to fix anything the inspection finds. A cleaner who inspects the tenancy first can tell you how long it will take.

## What should you ask the contractor?

Ask for a written scope and price for the handover alone, whether they will return to fix items found at inspection and whether the work will be photographed.
