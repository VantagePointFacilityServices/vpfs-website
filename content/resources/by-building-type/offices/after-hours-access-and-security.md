When cleaners work in an office after hours, you manage the risk with a written access arrangement: who holds keys and codes, how access is recorded, how alarms and doors are handled, what areas are off limits and who is responsible for losses or breaches. Agree these before the first clean, not after the first problem.

## Why after-hours cleaning needs rules

Most office cleaning happens when the building is empty, which is good for staff but means cleaners have unsupervised access to your space, your equipment and sometimes your records. Clear rules protect you, and they protect the cleaners from being blamed for something they did not do.

## What should the access arrangement cover?

- **Keys, fobs and codes.** Who holds them, how they are issued and returned, and whether codes are individual or shared. Individual codes let you see who was in and when.
- **Alarm procedure.** How cleaners arm and disarm, and what to do if it triggers.
- **Entry and exit.** Which doors, how they are secured on departure and who locks up.
- **Off-limits areas.** Server rooms, records rooms, executive offices or anywhere cleaners must not enter, and how those are locked.
- **Sensitive material.** Clear-desk expectations so confidential paper is not left out.
- **Visitor and tenant rules.** Building management requirements in multi-tenant buildings.

## What should you ask the contractor?

Ask how their staff are screened and inducted for your site, who the named people are, how keys and codes are controlled and what happens if a key is lost. Ask what insurance covers and read the contract on responsibility. Our guide to [questions to ask a commercial cleaner](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/choosing-and-switching/questions-to-ask-a-commercial-cleaner/) lists more.

## What should the contract say?

State who is authorised to enter, require the contractor to report lost keys or incidents immediately and require return of keys and codes on exit. See [cleaning contract terms to check](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/cleaning-contract-terms-to-check/).

## What about safety for cleaners?

Cleaners working alone at night should have a clear way to raise an alarm and a contact for emergencies. As the engaging business you share responsibility for a safe site, so check that induction covers emergency exits, lighting and who to call.
