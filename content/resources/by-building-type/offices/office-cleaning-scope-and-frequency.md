A good office cleaning scope lists each area, the tasks in it and a frequency for each task, with high-touch and high-traffic items done every visit and periodic items done weekly, monthly or quarterly. Setting frequencies task by task, not one blanket frequency for everything, is what keeps the price fair and the standard consistent.

## What areas does an office scope cover?

Most office scopes cover reception and entry, open-plan workstations, private offices, meeting rooms, kitchens and break rooms, washrooms, corridors and lift lobbies, and internal glass. Add anything specific to your site, such as a server room that must not be entered, a training room or an end-of-trip facility.

## Which tasks belong at which frequency?

| Frequency | Typical office tasks |
|---|---|
| Every visit | Empty bins, clean and restock washrooms, wipe kitchen benches and sinks, clean entry glass, spot-vacuum high-traffic areas, wipe high-touch surfaces |
| Weekly | Full vacuum of all floors, mop hard floors, wipe desks that are clear, clean meeting-room tables, dust accessible surfaces |
| Monthly | Internal glass and partitions, skirting and edges, appliance exteriors, detailed washroom work |
| Quarterly or as needed | High dusting, vents, carpet extraction, hard-floor restoration |

These are examples, not a standard. Your frequencies should reflect how busy the space is and how it looks to clients and staff.

## What should you decide about desks?

Cleaners normally wipe desks only if they are clear. Decide whether staff are expected to clear desks and say so in the scope. Otherwise "clean desks" becomes a dispute.

## How does headcount affect the scope?

A fuller office needs more attention than an empty one of the same size, particularly in kitchens, washrooms and high-touch areas. Tell contractors the number of staff and how many are in on a typical day. See [what affects a cleaning quote](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/what-affects-a-cleaning-quote/).

## What are common mistakes?

Asking for every task every night is the most common, and it inflates the price. The second is leaving out exclusions, such as personal items, confidential areas and IT equipment. The third is not stating who restocks consumables.
