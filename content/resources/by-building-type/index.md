The right commercial cleaning scope depends on the building. An office, a strata complex, a medical practice, a school and a warehouse all need cleaning, but they differ in who uses the space, when it can be cleaned, what risks it carries and what a good result looks like. A scope copied from one building type to another is the most common reason cleaning disappoints.

This guide is for the facility manager, property manager, committee member or practice owner who has to specify cleaning for a particular kind of building. Pick your building type below and you will find what is different about it, what to put in the scope and what to ask a contractor.

## How do building types differ?

Four things change most from one building type to the next. The first is who is in the building and when, which sets the hours the cleaners can work. The second is the risk profile: infection control in a clinic, children's safety in a school, forklifts and dust in a warehouse. The third is the standard people expect to see, such as presentation in a lobby or hygiene in a washroom. The fourth is who is accountable, whether that is a tenant, a strata committee, a practice owner, a principal or a site manager.

## What do all building types have in common?

Whatever the building, the same process applies: write a scope of work, get quotes against it, check the contractor's people, insurance and quality checks, and put the scope in the contract.
