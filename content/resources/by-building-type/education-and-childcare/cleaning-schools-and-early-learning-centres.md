For a school or early-learning centre, specify the scope room by room, set cleaning hours that avoid children's hours, follow current health guidance for infection control, require the worker screening your service needs, and agree how the cleaner reports hazards. The standard is shaped by who is in the building.

## What makes education sites different?

Children touch everything and spread illness quickly. Classrooms, play areas, toilets and shared equipment all need attention to hygiene. The hours are set by the timetable, and a clean that was done at night must still be fine when the children arrive in the morning.

## What does health guidance say?

For early childhood services, the NHMRC publishes *Staying healthy: Preventing infectious diseases in early childhood education and care services*, which covers hygiene and cleaning practices. See the [NHMRC infection prevention page](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/health-advice/public-health/preventing-infection). Schools and services should also follow guidance from their own education authority. Use these as the reference for what your service requires.

## What should the scope cover?

Specify areas such as classrooms, toilets and nappy-change areas, kitchens and food-preparation areas, play and sleep areas, staff rooms, offices, halls and corridors. List high-touch surfaces separately and give each a frequency: door handles, taps, bubblers and shared tables are the usual candidates. In early-learning centres, state how toys, mats and cots are handled and by whom, since many are cleaned by educators as part of care.

## What about screening and safety?

Check what worker screening your service or education authority requires for contractors on site and ask the contractor to confirm in writing that their staff meet it. Induct cleaners on emergency procedures, secure areas and chemical storage. Chemicals must be kept out of children's reach, and safety data sheets must be available.

## When should cleaning happen?

Most routine cleaning happens after hours. Where cleaning must happen during operation, such as toilets during the day, agree how it is done safely and with minimal disruption.

## How should the contractor report problems?

Ask them to report hazards, damage or health concerns they notice, such as broken fixtures or suspected vermin, to a named contact straight away.
