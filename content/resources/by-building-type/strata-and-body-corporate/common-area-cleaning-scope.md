A strata common-area cleaning scope should list every shared space the body corporate pays to have cleaned, the tasks in each, how often each is done and what is excluded. Because the people who approve the contract are not the people who inspect the result, the scope needs to be specific enough for anyone to check.

## Which areas are common property?

Typically lobbies and entry foyers, lifts, corridors, stairwells, mail areas, bin rooms and chutes, car parks, shared amenities and any gym, pool-side or function areas. Check your plan and by-laws so the scope matches what the body corporate actually maintains, and leave out areas that belong to lot owners.

## What tasks and frequencies suit common areas?

| Area | Typical tasks | Typical frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Entry and lobby | Sweep or vacuum, mop, clean glass and doors, wipe mailboxes | Several times a week |
| Lifts | Wipe walls, buttons and handrails, clean floor and tracks | Several times a week |
| Corridors and stairwells | Vacuum or mop, spot-clean, dust handrails | Weekly |
| Bin rooms and chutes | Clear, wash down, deodorise | Weekly |
| Car park | Sweep, spot-clean marks | Fortnightly or monthly |
| Amenities | Full clean, restock | Per use pattern |
| Glass and high dusting | Internal glass, cobwebs, vents | Monthly or quarterly |

Treat this as a starting point. Frequencies should follow use: a large building with heavy foot traffic needs more than a small low-rise.

## What should be excluded or priced separately?

State what is not included, such as pressure washing, graffiti removal, carpet extraction, external windows at height and garden or pool care. These are often separate trades and separate quotes.

## How do you keep it readable for a committee?

Use a one-page table by area, plain language and a short list of exclusions. Attach it as a schedule to the contract. Avoid jargon, so committee members and new owners can see what is being delivered.
