A strata committee should evaluate cleaning contractors by writing one scope, getting several written quotes against it, checking each contractor's insurance and people, scoring the quotes on agreed criteria and recording the reasons for the decision, then seeking whatever approval your scheme's rules require. The paper trail is as important as the choice.

## Check the rules for your scheme first

Who can approve a contract, and up to what value, depends on your scheme's rules, by-laws and any resolutions passed by owners. In Queensland, the Government's page on [service contractors](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/body-corporate/maintenance/service-contractors/role) defines a service contractor as someone engaged by a body corporate to supply services, other than administrative services, for at least a year. Ask your body corporate manager how a cleaning engagement is classified in your scheme and what approval it needs.

## Step 1: Write one scope

Every contractor should price the same thing. Use the common-area scope you have agreed. See [common-area cleaning scope](/resources/by-building-type/strata-and-body-corporate/common-area-cleaning-scope/).

## Step 2: Get several quotes

Ask for written quotes from a small number of contractors who work in buildings like yours. Offer each the same site inspection and give the same answers to questions.

## Step 3: Check the contractor, not only the price

Ask for current insurance certificates, how cleaners are engaged and paid, who supervises and what references they can give from other strata buildings. Our [questions to ask a commercial cleaner](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/choosing-and-switching/questions-to-ask-a-commercial-cleaner/) lists them.

## Step 4: Compare and score

Lay the quotes side by side using [how to compare cleaning quotes like for like](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/compare-cleaning-quotes/) and the [tender scorecard](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/tenders-and-rfqs/cleaning-tender-evaluation-scorecard/). Scoring protects volunteer committee members, because the decision rests on criteria agreed in advance.

## Step 5: Get approval and record it

Take the recommendation, the comparison and the contract to whichever meeting or body must approve it, and record the decision in the minutes with the reasons. Keep the quotes, scorecard and certificates with the body corporate records.

## Step 6: Plan reporting and review

Agree how the contractor will report to the committee and when the contract will be reviewed. See [reporting to a strata committee](/resources/by-building-type/strata-and-body-corporate/reporting-to-a-strata-committee/).
