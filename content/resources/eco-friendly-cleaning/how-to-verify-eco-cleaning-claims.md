To verify a commercial cleaner's eco claims in a tender or quote, ask for the product list, the independent certification for each product and the safety data sheet, then check that the written scope matches what they say. A contractor who is genuinely using greener products can hand these over the same day.

General information for buyers. Check ecolabel and regulator pages for the current position before you rely on them.

## Step 1: Ask for the product list

Ask which products will be used in your building and for which tasks. A vague answer such as "we use green products" is not a list. You want product names, suppliers and the task each is used for. Note anything used for disinfection separately.

## Step 2: Check the certification

For each product ask who certified it, to which standard and under which licence. Independent ecolabels publish their standards and directories, so you can confirm a claim yourself. In Australia, GECA publishes a [directory of certified cleaning products](https://www.geca.eco/products/cleaning-products) and its [cleaning products standard](https://www.geca.eco/cleaning-products-cpv3-0-2022). Match the product name and the licence holder to the directory. Certification covers the specific product, not the whole company, so a certified product range does not make every product in the cart certified. See [ecolabels and certifications explained](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/understanding-eco-claims/ecolabels-and-certifications-explained/).

## Step 3: Read the safety data sheet

Every hazardous chemical used at work should have a safety data sheet. It tells you what is in the product and how it must be used. If a contractor cannot produce one, treat that as a warning about their wider systems. See [how to read a safety data sheet](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/chemistry/reading-a-safety-data-sheet/).

## Step 4: Match the claim to the scope

Greener products only help if they are written into the scope. Check that your agreement names the products or product categories and says when a disinfectant is used. See [writing eco requirements into a cleaning scope](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/in-practice/writing-eco-requirements-into-a-cleaning-scope/).

## Step 5: Check the provider as well as the product

Some contractors are certified as a service, which looks at training, waste, water and supply chain practices as well as products. GECA has a cleaning services standard for this. If a contractor says they are certified, ask which standard and ask to see the certificate.

## Step 6: Watch for absolute claims

Be cautious of claims such as "chemical-free" or "completely safe". Water is a chemical, and almost any cleaning product can cause harm if misused. The ACCC's guidance on environmental claims warns against broad, unqualified claims and says claims need evidence. Credible contractors talk about reducing risk, not removing it. See [greenwashing red flags in cleaning tenders](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/understanding-eco-claims/greenwashing-red-flags-in-cleaning-tenders/).

## Step 7: Record what you were told

Keep the product list, certificates and sheets with your contractor documents so you can check them again at renewal. See [documents a contractor should give you](/resources/compliance-and-risk/records-and-reporting/documents-a-contractor-should-give-you/).
