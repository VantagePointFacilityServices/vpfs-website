To verify a commercial cleaner's eco claims in a tender or quote, ask for the product list, the independent certification for each product and the safety data sheet, then check that the written scope matches what they say. A contractor who is genuinely using greener products can hand these over the same day.

## Step 1: Ask for the product list

Ask which products will be used in your building and for which tasks. A vague answer such as "we use green products" is not a list. You want product names, not a brand promise.

## Step 2: Check the certification

For each product, ask who certified it. Independent ecolabels such as [GECA](https://www.geca.eco/) publish their standards and directories, so you can confirm a claim yourself instead of taking the contractor's word.

## Step 3: Read the safety data sheet

Every hazardous chemical used at work should have a safety data sheet. It tells you what is in the product and how it must be used. If a contractor cannot produce one, treat that as a warning about their wider systems.

## Step 4: Match the claim to the scope

Greener products only help if they are written into the scope. Check that your agreement names the products or product categories and says when a disinfectant is used. Our own approach to scopes is on the [overview of eco-friendly cleaning](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/).

## Step 5: Watch for absolute claims

Be cautious of claims such as "chemical-free" or "completely safe". Water is a chemical, and almost any cleaning product can cause harm if misused. Credible contractors talk about reducing risk, not removing it.
