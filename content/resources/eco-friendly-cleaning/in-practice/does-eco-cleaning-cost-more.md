Eco-friendly cleaning can cost more, the same or less than conventional cleaning, depending on what you specify and how the contractor works. Labour is the largest cost in cleaning, and product is a small part of it, so a green requirement usually moves the price less than frequency, scope or access hours. Compare quotes against the same written requirements to see the real difference.

General information for buyers. Check ecolabel and regulator pages for the current position before you rely on them.

## Where could it cost more?

- **Certified products:** some cost more per litre than the cheapest conventional alternatives.
- **Provider certification:** if you require a contractor to be certified as a service, the contractor carries assessment and administration costs.
- **Different methods:** a method that takes longer, for example more contact time or more frequent passes, adds labour.
- **Trial and transition:** changing products can require retraining and testing.

## Where could it cost less?

- **Concentrates and dosing:** correct dilution can cut the volume of product used.
- **Reusable cloths:** less spend on disposables.
- **Machines:** efficient use of water and solution on large floors.
- **Fewer problems:** lower-fume products can mean fewer complaints.

These are possibilities, not promises. We have not quoted percentages here because we have not found a sound published source for them, and figures in supplier marketing are often unsupported.

## Why does product matter less than labour?

Most of a cleaning price is the time of the people doing the work. See [how commercial cleaning is priced](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/how-commercial-cleaning-is-priced/). A requirement that does not change the time on site often changes the price only modestly. A requirement that adds tasks or visits changes it more.

## How do you compare quotes fairly?

Specify the green requirements in the scope and ask every contractor to price the same requirements. Ask each to show the product cost separately if it is a meaningful part of their price. Check that a lower quote is not lower because it leaves the requirement out. See [how to compare cleaning quotes like for like](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/compare-cleaning-quotes/).

## How do you decide what is worth paying for?

Be clear about what you want and why: resident or staff comfort, an organisational policy or a tender requirement. Put a value on it. Then decide whether the extra cost, if any, is justified by the benefit. Our guide to [writing eco requirements into a cleaning scope](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/in-practice/writing-eco-requirements-into-a-cleaning-scope/) helps you choose which requirements to specify.
