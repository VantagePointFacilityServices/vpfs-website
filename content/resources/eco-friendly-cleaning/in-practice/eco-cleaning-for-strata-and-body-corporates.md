For a strata or body corporate, eco-friendly cleaning is a request that has to be specific, defensible to owners and easy to check. Decide what you want, such as certified products in common areas and less fragrance, write it into the scope, and ask every contractor to quote against the same requirements.

General information for buyers. Check ecolabel and regulator pages for the current position before you rely on them.

## Why do committees ask for it?

Residents and owners notice smells, residues and chemical use in shared spaces where they live, including lobbies, lifts and corridors. Many also care about environmental impact. A committee that can show owners what products are used, and why, has a clear answer when questions come.

## What should you decide first?

Decide what "eco-friendly" means for your scheme, in practical terms. Options include certified products for general cleaning, fragrance-free or low-fragrance products in lifts and lobbies, reusable cloths and recycling of packaging. Not every item will matter to your owners, and every item may affect price.

## How do you write it into the scope?

Use the same method as for any commercial site: put the requirements in the scope, list the evidence you want and reserve the right to check. See [writing eco requirements into a cleaning scope](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/in-practice/writing-eco-requirements-into-a-cleaning-scope/) and [common-area cleaning scope](/resources/by-building-type/strata-and-body-corporate/common-area-cleaning-scope/).

## How do you compare quotes fairly?

Send every contractor the same requirements and ask each to name its products and certification. Score the answers with the committee scorecard so the decision is defensible. See [how committees evaluate cleaning contractors](/resources/by-building-type/strata-and-body-corporate/how-committees-evaluate-cleaning-contractors/) and [how to verify a contractor's eco claims](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/how-to-verify-eco-cleaning-claims/).

## What about resident communication?

If you are making a point of using certified products, tell residents what is used and why, and be careful with wording. Claims made to residents or in marketing for the building should be accurate and supported by evidence. The ACCC's guidance on environmental claims is a useful reference. See [greenwashing red flags in cleaning tenders](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/understanding-eco-claims/greenwashing-red-flags-in-cleaning-tenders/).

## What about reporting?

Ask the contractor to include a product check in its regular report, and to flag any change. See [reporting to a strata committee](/resources/by-building-type/strata-and-body-corporate/reporting-to-a-strata-committee/).
