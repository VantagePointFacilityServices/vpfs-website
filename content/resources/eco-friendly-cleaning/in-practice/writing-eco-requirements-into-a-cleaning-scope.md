To make eco-friendly cleaning enforceable, write specific, checkable requirements into the scope and contract: which products or certifications are required, what documents must be supplied, how dosing and waste are managed and how the contractor reports. Replace "eco-friendly" with things you can inspect.

General information for buyers. Check ecolabel and regulator pages for the current position before you rely on them.

## Why not just ask for "green cleaning"?

Because the phrase has no fixed meaning, the contractor decides what it means. Your requirement should say what you actually want, in terms that can be verified.

## What requirements can you set?

| Area | Example requirement |
|---|---|
| Product certification | General-purpose and washroom products certified to an independent ecolabel standard, with the product list and certificates supplied before the start date |
| Safety data sheets | A current sheet for every product used on site, updated when products change |
| Product changes | No new product on site without prior written approval |
| Disinfectants | Used only where specified, with the product named and the reason recorded |
| Dosing | Measured dilution through dosing units or pre-measured product |
| Equipment | Reusable, colour-coded cloths and mop heads, laundered off site or on site as agreed |
| Waste | Recycling of packaging where schemes exist, and no disposal of chemicals to stormwater |
| Provider certification | Optional: contractor certified to a recognised cleaning services standard |
| Reporting | An annual review of products and certificates against the contract |

Adapt these to your site and policy. Check what your procurement rules accept as certification.

## How should you word it?

Be specific and measurable. For example: "The contractor will supply a product list and independent certification evidence for each general-purpose and washroom product before the commencement date, and will notify the client in writing before introducing any new product." Avoid unqualified claims such as "all products will be environmentally friendly".

## What should the contract allow you to do?

Reserve the right to inspect products and sheets on site, to request evidence at any time and to require changes if a product is not as stated. Include a remedy, such as correction within a set time, if requirements are not met. See [cleaning contract terms to check](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/cleaning-contract-terms-to-check/).

## How do you check it?

Include product checks in your regular inspection and keep the file up to date. See [documents a contractor should give you](/resources/compliance-and-risk/records-and-reporting/documents-a-contractor-should-give-you/) and [how cleaning quality is audited](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/how-cleaning-quality-is-audited/).
