An ecolabel is an independent certification that a product or service has been assessed against a published environmental standard. In Australian commercial cleaning the one to know is GECA, which certifies both cleaning products and cleaning services. Overseas labels such as Green Seal, the EU Ecolabel and the Nordic Swan also exist, but you should check each claim against the certifier's own directory.

General information for buyers. Check ecolabel and regulator pages for the current position before you rely on them.

## What makes an ecolabel credible?

A credible ecolabel has a published standard, independent assessment of products against it, a public directory you can search and a licence or certificate number for each certified product. Many logos on packaging are self-declared, which means the manufacturer made them up or paid for a badge without an assessment. Check who runs the label and whether the standard is public.

## What is GECA?

Good Environmental Choice Australia describes itself as Australia's not-for-profit, multi-sector ecolabelling program, working to ISO 14024 principles. It has [a standard for cleaning products](https://www.geca.eco/cleaning-products-cpv3-0-2022) that covers general purpose cleaners, sanitary cleaners, laundry and dishwashing products. It limits volatile organic compounds, restricts substances harmful to aquatic life and prohibits certain hazardous ingredients such as carcinogens, mutagens and reproductive toxins. It also places limits on fragrances and irritants and looks at packaging and supply chains. Industrial and institutional products can be certified if they meet the standard. Products designed to change air quality, such as standalone air fresheners, are excluded.

## Can a service be certified?

Yes. GECA also has a [cleaning services standard](https://www.geca.eco/cleaning-services-csv2-0-2021) for providers. It looks at procurement of environmentally preferable products, training in chemical handling, waste and reuse, responsible water use and fair pay and safe conditions through the supply chain. Certification of a service tells you about the contractor's systems, not just one bottle. Ask a contractor whether they hold it and, if so, to see the certificate.

## What about overseas labels?

Green Seal (United States), the EU Ecolabel, the Nordic Swan, EcoLogo and the US EPA Safer Choice label all certify cleaning products to their own standards. Some products sold in Australia carry them. Treat them the same way: check the certifier's directory for the specific product, and ask how the standard compares with the one you care about. We have not confirmed how widely any of these is recognised in Australian tenders, so check what your own procurement policy accepts.

## How do you check a claim?

1. Note the exact product name and the label or licence number.
2. Search the certifier's directory. For GECA, use the [certified product directory](https://www.geca.eco/products/cleaning-products).
3. Confirm the licence holder, the product and that the certificate is current.
4. Check which standard and version it was certified to.
5. Keep a copy with your contractor documents.

## What does a label not tell you?

It does not tell you the product suits your surfaces or soils, that it disinfects, or that the contractor uses it correctly. It also does not cover every product the contractor uses. Disinfectant claims are regulated separately by the TGA, as explained in [cleaner vs sanitiser vs disinfectant](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/chemistry/cleaner-vs-sanitiser-vs-disinfectant/).
