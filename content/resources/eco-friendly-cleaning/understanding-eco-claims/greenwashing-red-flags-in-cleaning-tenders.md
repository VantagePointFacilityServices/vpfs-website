The main greenwashing red flags in a cleaning tender are vague or absolute words, claims with no evidence, logos you cannot trace to a certifier and green claims that are not reflected in the scope or contract. Each can be tested with a simple question, and a contractor with nothing to hide will answer it.

General information for buyers. Check ecolabel and regulator pages for the current position before you rely on them.

## Why does this matter to you?

If you accept a claim and repeat it, for example in a sustainability report or to owners, you are relying on it. In Australia the ACCC publishes guidance on [making environmental claims](https://www.accc.gov.au/about-us/publications/making-environmental-claims-a-guide-for-business). Its principles include making accurate claims, having evidence, not hiding important information, explaining conditions and avoiding broad, unqualified claims. Those same principles make a good checklist for the claims you are shown.

## What are the red flags?

| Red flag | Example | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Vague words | "Green", "natural", "eco" with no detail | What exactly is green about it, and how do you know? |
| Absolute claims | "Chemical-free", "non-toxic", "100% safe" | Which standard or test supports this, and under what conditions? |
| Logos you cannot trace | A leaf or globe with no certifier named | Who awards the logo and can I see the certificate? |
| Whole-company claims | "We are a green company" | Which products and practices, and what evidence? |
| No product list | "We use only eco products" | Which products, for which tasks, with safety data sheets? |
| Fragrance as proof | A pleasant smell offered as evidence of clean or safe | What is the product and what does the safety data sheet say? |
| Green claim missing from the contract | Strong in the proposal, silent in the scope | Will you write it into the agreement? |
| Hidden trade-offs | Eco product that needs more product or more time | What is the overall effect on chemical use, waste and labour? |

## How do you test a claim?

Ask for the evidence in writing: product list, certificates, safety data sheets and, for service claims, the certificate for the provider. Check certificates against the certifier's directory. See [ecolabels and certifications explained](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/understanding-eco-claims/ecolabels-and-certifications-explained/). Ask how the claim will be maintained over the contract, for example if products change.

## What if the answers are weak?

A weak answer is information. Score it accordingly in your [tender scorecard](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/tenders-and-rfqs/cleaning-tender-evaluation-scorecard/), or ask for clarification before you decide. Do not accept an eco claim you cannot verify as a reason to pay more.
