For a facility manager or strata committee, eco-friendly commercial cleaning means your contractor uses products, equipment and methods that reduce harm to people and the environment while still getting the building properly clean. It is a set of trade-offs, not a single product, and the claims in a tender response need checking before you score them.

## What "eco-friendly" does and does not tell you

The phrase has no fixed legal meaning on a product label. A cleaner can call itself green, natural or eco without any independent check behind it. What matters is whether a third party has assessed the product against a published standard, such as the ecolabel programs run by [GECA](https://www.geca.eco/), and whether the contractor can show you which of their products carry that certification.

## Where greener cleaning works well

Routine daily and weekly cleaning is where greener products earn their place: general surface cleaning, glass, washrooms and floor care. These are the tasks done in occupied buildings, so lower-fume products and fewer harsh residues are a real benefit to the people working there.

## Where you still need a stronger product

Some jobs need a disinfectant or a specialist chemical. Clinical areas, outbreak response and heavily soiled washrooms are the usual examples. A good contractor uses the right product for the risk and tells you when and why. Be wary of any supplier who says they never need one.

## Questions to ask a cleaning contractor

- Which of your products are independently certified, and by whom?
- Can I see the safety data sheet for each product used on my site?
- How do you dilute and dose, and how do you avoid over-use?
- Which tasks do you use a disinfectant for, and why?
- How do you handle waste and packaging?
