For a facility manager or strata committee, eco-friendly commercial cleaning means your contractor uses products, equipment and methods that reduce harm to people and the environment while still getting the building properly clean. It is a set of trade-offs, not a single product, and the claims in a tender response need checking before you score them.

General information for buyers. Check ecolabel and regulator pages for the current position before you rely on them.

## What does "eco-friendly" actually tell you?

Very little on its own. In Australia the phrase has no fixed meaning on a product label, so a cleaner can call itself green, natural or eco without any independent check behind it. What carries weight is evidence: an independent ecolabel assessed against a published standard, safety data sheets that show what is in the product and a contractor who can explain how they use it. The ACCC's guidance on environmental claims sets out what businesses must do to avoid misleading buyers, and the same principles are a useful test for any claim you are shown. See [greenwashing red flags in cleaning tenders](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/understanding-eco-claims/greenwashing-red-flags-in-cleaning-tenders/).

## What independent certification exists in Australia?

Good Environmental Choice Australia (GECA) runs Australia's not-for-profit, multi-sector ecolabelling program. It has a standard for cleaning products and a separate standard for cleaning services, so you can ask about the product, the provider or both. Overseas programs such as Green Seal, the EU Ecolabel and the Nordic Swan also certify cleaning products. [Ecolabels and certifications explained](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/understanding-eco-claims/ecolabels-and-certifications-explained/) shows how to tell them apart and how to check one.

## Where does greener cleaning work well?

Routine daily and weekly cleaning is where greener products earn their place: general surface cleaning, glass, washrooms and floor care. These are the tasks done in occupied buildings, so lower-fume products and fewer harsh residues are a benefit to the people working there. Methods matter as much as products. Correct dosing, reusable cloths and machines that use less water all cut the amount of chemical and waste. See [how dosing and equipment cut chemical use](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/products-and-methods/dosing-and-equipment-to-cut-chemical-use/).

## Where do you still need a stronger product?

Some jobs need a disinfectant or a specialist chemical: clinical areas, outbreak response and heavily soiled washrooms are the usual examples. A good contractor uses the right product for the risk and tells you when and why. Be wary of a supplier who says they never need one. Disinfectant claims are regulated separately from cleaning claims, as explained in [cleaner vs sanitiser vs disinfectant](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/chemistry/cleaner-vs-sanitiser-vs-disinfectant/).

## How do you choose between product types?

Plant-based, enzymatic and conventional cleaners each have a place, and each is sold with strong marketing. The sensible test is performance on your soils and surfaces, a safety data sheet and, where it exists, independent certification. See [plant-based, enzymatic and conventional cleaners compared](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/products-and-methods/plant-based-enzymatic-and-conventional-cleaners/).

## How do you put it into a contract?

Write your requirements into the scope: certified products where you want them, the right to see safety data sheets, dosing and waste expectations and what the contractor must report. See [writing eco requirements into a cleaning scope](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/in-practice/writing-eco-requirements-into-a-cleaning-scope/), [eco cleaning for strata and body corporates](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/in-practice/eco-cleaning-for-strata-and-body-corporates/) and [does eco cleaning cost more?](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/in-practice/does-eco-cleaning-cost-more/).

## Questions to ask a cleaning contractor

- Which of your products are independently certified, by whom and to which standard?
- Can I see the safety data sheet for every product used on my site?
- How do you dilute and dose, and how do you avoid over-use?
- Which tasks do you use a disinfectant for, and why?
- How do you handle waste and packaging?
