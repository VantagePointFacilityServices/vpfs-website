The biggest practical way to reduce the chemicals a cleaning contract uses is to dose them correctly. Measured dilution, reusable cloths, the right machine for the floor and trained staff cut chemical use, water and waste whatever products are chosen. Ask your contractor how they control each, because over-use is common and costly.

General information for buyers. Check ecolabel and regulator pages for the current position before you rely on them.

## Why is over-use a problem?

More product does not mean cleaner. Too much leaves residue that attracts dirt, wastes money, increases fumes and exposure and adds to what goes down the drain. Products are designed to work at a set dilution, so the safety data sheet and label should say what that is.

## What measures cut chemical use?

- **Measured dilution:** wall-mounted dosing units, pre-measured sachets or marked bottles so staff do not guess.
- **Concentrates:** buying concentrated products and diluting on site cuts packaging and transport.
- **Right product for the job:** using a mild product where it will do and a stronger one only where needed.
- **Reusable cloths and mop heads:** laundered and colour-coded, which cuts disposable waste. See [microfibre vs cotton](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/equipment/microfibre-vs-cotton/).
- **Machines where they suit:** an auto-scrubber on a large floor applies solution and recovers it, using it efficiently. See [auto-scrubbers vs mopping](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/equipment/auto-scrubbers-vs-mopping/).
- **Training:** staff who know the dilution, the contact time and why not to mix products.
- **Waste handling:** recycling and returning containers where schemes exist.

## What does a certified service look at?

GECA's cleaning services standard covers procurement of environmentally preferable products, training in chemical handling, waste reduction and reuse and responsible water use. A contractor certified to it has been assessed on these practices. See [ecolabels and certifications explained](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/understanding-eco-claims/ecolabels-and-certifications-explained/).

## What should you ask a contractor?

How are products diluted and who checks it? Do you use dosing units or concentrates? Are cloths reusable and how are they laundered? What do you do with empty containers? How do you train staff and how often?

## How do you check it is happening?

Look at how products are stored and dispensed on site, whether bottles are labelled and whether dosing equipment is used. Ask for training records and include chemical use in your regular review. See [safety data sheets and chemical management](/resources/compliance-and-risk/what-a-compliant-contractor-holds/safety-data-sheets-and-chemical-management/).
