Plant-based, enzymatic and conventional cleaners all have a place in commercial cleaning. The right choice depends on the soil, the surface and the risk, not on the label. Marketing for all three is strong, so the best test is the safety data sheet, any independent certification and a trial on your own site.

General information for buyers. Check ecolabel and regulator pages for the current position before you rely on them.

## What are the three types?

- **Conventional cleaners** use synthetic detergents, solvents or other chemicals. They are the largest category and are available across the range from mild to very strong.
- **Plant-based cleaners** use ingredients derived from plants, such as certain surfactants and solvents. "Plant-based" describes the source of some ingredients, not the safety or the environmental impact of the whole product.
- **Enzymatic cleaners** contain enzymes, which are biological catalysts that break down specific organic soils such as fats, proteins and starches. Some products also contain live microbes and are sold as biological or probiotic cleaners.

## Where does each work well?

| Type | Often suits | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional | Heavy soil, scale, grease, disinfection where required | Fumes, hazard to users, the need for the right protective equipment |
| Plant-based | Routine cleaning of surfaces, glass, floors | The label tells you the source of some ingredients only. Check the sheet and any certification |
| Enzymatic | Organic soils and odours, drains, washroom maintenance, kitchens | Performance depends on contact time and conditions. Not a disinfectant unless it is registered as one |

## What does the evidence say?

Searches for enzymatic and probiotic cleaners turn up mostly supplier marketing. We have not found independent comparative studies that settle how they perform against conventional products on commercial soils. Treat claims such as "digests" or "eliminates" as claims to test, not facts. Ask for performance data, a safety data sheet and a trial on a real area of your building.

## Does a certified product have to be plant-based or enzymatic?

No. Ecolabels such as GECA certify products against criteria on ingredients, emissions and aquatic harm, whatever the technology. Some certified commercial products include enzymatic or biologically active cleaners, and many are conventional detergents formulated to meet the standard. See [ecolabels and certifications explained](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/understanding-eco-claims/ecolabels-and-certifications-explained/).

## How do you decide?

Ask what soil you are dealing with and what the contractor proposes for it. Ask for the safety data sheet and any certification. Run a trial in a washroom or kitchen and check the result after a few visits. Make sure the product is suitable for your surfaces. See [neutral, alkaline and acid cleaners](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/chemistry/neutral-alkaline-and-acid-cleaners/).

## When do you still need something stronger?

Disinfection and heavy-duty jobs often need a product registered or formulated for it. See [cleaner vs sanitiser vs disinfectant](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/chemistry/cleaner-vs-sanitiser-vs-disinfectant/).
