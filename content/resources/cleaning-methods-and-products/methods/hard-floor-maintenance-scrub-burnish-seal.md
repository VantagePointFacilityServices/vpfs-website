Hard-floor maintenance has a daily routine of sweeping and mopping, with machine scrubbing for larger floors, and periodic restorative work such as burnishing, sealing or stripping and recoating. The right mix depends on the floor type, so identify the floor and ask for a method suited to it before you agree a scope.

This is written for the person buying or managing cleaning, so you can understand and check what a contractor is doing. It is not a training manual for cleaners.

## What are the main stages?

- **Dust control:** sweeping or dust-mopping removes grit that would otherwise scratch the surface.
- **Mopping:** cleans the surface with a suitable product, using a method that does not spread dirt around. Flat mops and clean solution are common in commercial settings.
- **Machine scrubbing:** an auto-scrubber applies solution, scrubs and picks up the dirty water in one pass, and is used on larger areas. See [auto-scrubbers vs mopping](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/equipment/auto-scrubbers-vs-mopping/).
- **Burnishing:** polishing a coated floor with a high-speed machine to restore shine between recoats.
- **Sealing, stripping and recoating:** periodic restoration where the protective finish is removed and renewed, or a seal applied. It is project work, usually quoted separately.

## Does the floor type matter?

Very much. Vinyl, tiles, polished concrete, sealed timber and stone all need different products and methods. The wrong product can dull a finish, damage a seal or make the floor slippery. Tell contractors the floor types and ask them to confirm their approach for each. Check the manufacturer's advice where you have it.

## What about safety?

Wet floors are a slip risk. Ask how the contractor signs and barriers wet areas, how floors are left safe at the end of a visit and how spills are handled. As the occupier you share responsibility for a safe site. See [WHS duties when you hire a cleaning contractor](/resources/compliance-and-risk/your-duties-as-the-engaging-business/whs-duties-when-you-hire-a-cleaning-contractor/).

## What should the scope say?

List the floor types by area, the routine method and frequency, and any periodic restoration with a month attached. State who moves furniture and how access is arranged for restoration work.

## How do you check the result?

Look at the floor in different light, at the edges and corners, and for streaks, residue or build-up. Photograph problem areas. A well-maintained floor looks consistent and feels safe underfoot.
