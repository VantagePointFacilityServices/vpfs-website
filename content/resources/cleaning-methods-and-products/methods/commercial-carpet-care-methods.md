Commercial carpet care is a routine of regular vacuuming and prompt spot cleaning, plus a periodic deeper clean by extraction or a low-moisture method. The right deep-cleaning method depends on the carpet, how busy the space is and how quickly it must be back in use. Ask which method will be used and check it against the carpet manufacturer's guidance.

This is written for the person buying or managing cleaning, so you can understand and check what a contractor is doing. It is not a training manual for cleaners.

## What are the main methods?

- **Vacuuming:** the daily and weekly basis. It removes loose soil before it grinds into the fibres. Machine type matters. See [vacuums for commercial carpet](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/equipment/vacuums-for-commercial-carpet/).
- **Spot cleaning:** removing spills and marks quickly, with an appropriate product, before they set.
- **Hot water extraction:** often called steam cleaning. Water and cleaner are applied and then extracted, which gives a deep clean but leaves the carpet damp for a time.
- **Low-moisture methods:** such as encapsulation or bonnet cleaning, which use little water so the carpet is usable sooner. They suit regular maintenance in busy offices.

## How do you choose?

Consider the carpet type, the level of soiling, how often the space is used and how long it can be out of action. Heavy traffic areas such as entries and corridors need cleaning more often than quiet rooms. Check the manufacturer's care advice, since some warranties specify methods or intervals.

## What should the scope say?

State the method or the outcome expected, the frequency, which areas are included and what happens with furniture. For example, say whether the contractor moves furniture or cleans around it. Agree timing so carpets are dry before staff return.

## What should you ask a contractor?

Ask which method they propose and why, what equipment they use, how long drying takes, what products are used and whether they will test a small area first. Ask for before-and-after photos of the work.

## What can go wrong?

Over-wetting can cause odours and shrinkage, the wrong product can leave residue that attracts dirt and moving furniture back too early can leave marks. These are reasons to agree the method up front.
