A proper commercial washroom clean follows a fixed order: prepare and sign the area, remove waste, apply product to fixtures, clean from top to bottom and from clean to dirty areas, rinse and wipe, restock supplies, mop the floor and check the result. Doing it in the same order every time is what makes the standard consistent.

This is written for the person buying or managing cleaning, so you can understand and check what a contractor is doing. It is not a training manual for cleaners.

## What does a standard clean involve?

- **Prepare:** place wet-floor signs, put on protective equipment and check supplies.
- **Waste:** empty bins, including sanitary bins where provided, and replace liners.
- **Apply product:** to toilets, urinals and basins so it has time to work.
- **Surfaces top down:** mirrors, benches, dispensers, partitions, then fixtures, so dirt falls onto surfaces not yet cleaned.
- **Fixtures:** scrub and rinse toilets and urinals, clean basins and taps, wipe all touch points.
- **Restock:** toilet paper, soap and paper towel.
- **Floor:** sweep and mop, finishing at the door so you do not walk over the wet floor.
- **Check:** look and smell, fix anything missed, remove signs only when safe.

## How long should a washroom take?

Time is driven by the number of fixtures, not floor area. ISSA's method counts every toilet, urinal and basin as a fixture and allows about three minutes each. That is a floor for the estimate, not a ceiling, and sites with heavy use or older fittings can take longer. ## Why separate equipment?

Cloths, mops and buckets used in washrooms should not be used elsewhere. Many contractors colour-code equipment so washroom items stay in washrooms. Ask how it is done. In clinics and schools it is usually required. See [clinical and non-clinical zones](/resources/by-building-type/medical-and-dental/clinical-and-non-clinical-zones/).

## What should the scope say?

State each washroom, the number of fixtures, the tasks, the frequency and who restocks and orders supplies. Add a mid-day check where use is heavy. See [washroom standards and complaints](/resources/healthy-workplace/for-customers-and-visitors/washroom-standards-and-complaints/).

## How do you check a washroom?

Walk it as a user would: fixtures clean and free of marks, no smell, mirrors streak-free, supplies stocked, floor clean and dry. Photograph issues and raise them against the scope.
