A commercial cleaning schedule spreads tasks over time so that high-use items are cleaned every visit and slower-building ones are done weekly, monthly or periodically. This keeps the building consistently clean without paying for every task every night. A good schedule is written down, task by task, and ticked off on every visit.

This is written for the person buying or managing cleaning, so you can understand and check what a contractor is doing. It is not a training manual for cleaners.

## Why not clean everything every visit?

Time is the main cost in cleaning. Some things need attention every day, such as bins, washrooms and kitchens. Others, such as skirting boards, vents and internal glass, build up slowly and only need doing now and then. Setting each task at the right frequency is how a scope gets a good result at a fair price. See [how commercial cleaning is priced](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/how-commercial-cleaning-is-priced/).

## What does a typical schedule look like?

| Frequency | Examples of tasks |
|---|---|
| Every visit | Empty bins, clean and restock washrooms, wipe kitchen surfaces, spot-vacuum traffic areas, wipe high-touch surfaces, clean entry glass |
| Weekly | Vacuum all floors, mop hard floors, dust accessible surfaces, clean meeting-room tables |
| Monthly | Internal glass and partitions, skirting and edges, appliance exteriors, detailed washroom work |
| Quarterly or periodic | High dusting, vents and light fittings, carpet extraction, hard-floor restoration |

This is an example, not a standard. Frequencies should follow how your building is used.

## How should you set the frequencies?

Start from use. A full open-plan office needs more attention than a quiet one, and a washroom used by many people needs it every visit. Think about what visitors see and what staff complain about, and set those items higher. Then review after a few weeks, and adjust where tasks are being done too often or not often enough. See [office cleaning scope and frequency](/resources/by-building-type/offices/office-cleaning-scope-and-frequency/).

## How do you check the schedule is followed?

Ask for a checklist ticked on every visit, with photos for key areas, and a monthly report against the scope. See [how cleaning quality is audited](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/how-cleaning-quality-is-audited/).

## What should you avoid?

A single blanket frequency for the whole building, tasks with no frequency at all and periodic tasks that are listed but never scheduled. Put a date or a month against each periodic task so it happens.
