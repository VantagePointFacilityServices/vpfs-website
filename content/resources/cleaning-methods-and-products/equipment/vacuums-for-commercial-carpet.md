For commercial carpet, the vacuum matters as much as how often it is used. The main types are upright, backpack and wide-area vacuums, and they differ in how well they lift soil and capture fine dust. Ask your contractor what they use, how it is maintained and whether it has a filter suited to your environment.

This is written for the person buying or managing cleaning, so you can understand and check what a contractor is doing. It is not a training manual for cleaners.

## What are the main types?

| Type | Typical use | Strengths | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upright | Open carpeted areas such as offices and corridors | Brush action loosens soil from carpet, wide cleaning path | Harder to use on stairs, under furniture or above the floor |
| Backpack | Offices, tight spaces, stairs and above-floor surfaces | Quick, mobile, good for detail and edges | Less brush action on heavily soiled carpet |
| Wide-area or walk-behind | Large carpeted floors | Fast on big open areas | Needs space to manoeuvre and storage |

These are general points and models vary. A good contractor can explain why they have chosen what they use.

## Why does filtration matter?

A vacuum that blows fine dust back into the air defeats part of the purpose. Filters, bag condition and maintenance affect how well a machine captures dust. If air quality is a concern for your staff, ask about filtration. See [dust, carpets and indoor air quality](/resources/healthy-workplace/air-and-environment/dust-carpets-and-indoor-air-quality/).

## What does maintenance involve?

Bags or bins emptied before they are overfull, filters cleaned or replaced, brushes cleared of hair and thread and cables checked for safety. A poorly maintained vacuum cleans poorly and may be unsafe.

## What should you ask a contractor?

What type do you use at my site and why? Does it have a filter and how often is it changed? How do you clean edges, stairs and under furniture? How are machines maintained and tested for electrical safety?

## What about noise and hours?

Vacuums are loud. If staff or tenants are in the building, ask about quieter machines or timing. This is another reason to agree service hours up front. See [after-hours access and security](/resources/by-building-type/offices/after-hours-access-and-security/).

## What should the scope say?

State the carpeted areas, the frequency of vacuuming and which areas get edge and under-furniture cleaning. See [commercial carpet care methods](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/methods/commercial-carpet-care-methods/).
