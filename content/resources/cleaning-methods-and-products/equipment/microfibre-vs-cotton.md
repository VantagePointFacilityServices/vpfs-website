Microfibre and cotton are the two main materials for commercial cloths and mops. Microfibre is widely used for general cleaning because it picks up and holds dust and soil well, while cotton is still common for some mopping and heavy-duty tasks. What matters most is that the contractor uses clean, correctly laundered, colour-coded cloths in each area.

This is written for the person buying or managing cleaning, so you can understand and check what a contractor is doing. It is not a training manual for cleaners.

## How do they differ?

| | Microfibre | Cotton |
|---|---|---|
| Handling dust and soil | Tends to pick up and hold fine soil well | Absorbs liquid well, may spread soil if dirty |
| Typical uses | Surfaces, glass, general wiping, flat mops | Some wet mopping, heavy spills |
| Care | Needs correct laundering, and can lose effectiveness if washed wrongly | Durable, easy to launder |
| Cost | Often higher to buy | Often lower to buy |

These are general points, and individual products vary. Ask your contractor why they use what they use.

## What matters more than the material?

Cleanliness of the cloth. A dirty cloth spreads dirt, whatever it is made of. Cloths and mop heads need to be laundered after use and swapped out as they become soiled, so ask how often and where. Single-use or limited-use mop heads reduce cross-contamination risk, which is why they are common in washrooms and clinics.

## What about colour coding?

Many contractors use colour-coded cloths and buckets for different zones, for example one colour for washrooms, one for kitchens, one for general surfaces. It stops a washroom cloth being used on a kitchen bench. In clinics and schools it is often required. See [washroom cleaning standard](/resources/cleaning-methods-and-products/methods/washroom-cleaning-standard/).

## What should you ask?

Do you use colour-coded cloths and mops? How are they laundered and how often are they replaced? Do you use disposable cloths anywhere? How do you avoid cross-contamination between areas?

## Does this affect cost and the environment?

Reusable microfibre means fewer disposable products but needs laundering. Contractors who say one option is better for the environment should be able to explain why. See [how to verify a contractor's eco claims](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/how-to-verify-eco-cleaning-claims/).
