A cleaner removes dirt and soil, a sanitiser reduces germs to a safer level on surfaces, and a disinfectant is designed to kill specified germs when used as directed. Cleaning comes first, because dirt can stop disinfectants working. Most commercial areas need regular cleaning and only some need disinfection, so ask your contractor which products they use and why.

This is written for the person buying or managing cleaning, so you can understand and check what a contractor is doing. It is not a training manual for cleaners.

## What does each one do?

| Product | Main job | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaner (detergent) | Removes dirt, grease and soil | Everyday cleaning of floors, surfaces, kitchens and washrooms |
| Sanitiser | Reduces germs to a safer level | Food-contact surfaces, some washroom and kitchen uses |
| Disinfectant | Kills specified germs when used as directed | Clinical areas, washroom fixtures where required, outbreak response |

## How does Australia regulate them?

The Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) regulates disinfectants. According to its guidance on the [regulation of cleaners and disinfectants](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/resource/guidance/regulation-cleaners-and-disinfectants-information-sponsors-and-manufacturers), products that claim to kill or be active against specific organisms such as viruses, spores or fungi must be on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods before supply. General cleaners and sanitisers that make no disinfectant claims are generally not regulated by the TGA. Hospital grade and household or commercial grade disinfectants face different requirements. Read the TGA page for the current position.

## What should you take from this?

Be careful with marketing words such as "antibacterial" or "hospital grade". Ask for the product name, the label claims and the safety data sheet, and check the claims against what the product is regulated as. A claim on the label should match how the product is used.

## When does a commercial site need a disinfectant?

Many offices are well served by regular cleaning of surfaces with a detergent. The Queensland Government notes that flu viruses can be removed by this kind of cleaning. Disinfection is more likely to be needed in clinical areas, around an outbreak or where your own risk assessment says so. See [infection-control expectations in clinics](/resources/by-building-type/medical-and-dental/infection-control-expectations-in-clinics/) and [high-touch surfaces](/resources/healthy-workplace/for-staff/high-touch-surfaces-what-to-clean-and-how-often/).

## Why does the order matter?

Disinfectants work on clean surfaces. Applying one over visible dirt wastes product and may not give the result claimed. Products also need contact time, which means leaving them on the surface for the time on the label.

## What should you ask a contractor?

Which products do they use for general cleaning, which for disinfection, where is each used, how are they diluted and what contact time do they allow? Ask to see the labels and sheets.
