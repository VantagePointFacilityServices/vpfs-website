Agree before the contract starts how cleaners report hazards, near misses and incidents, who they tell and how fast, and how each report is recorded and closed out. Separately, serious incidents may have to be notified to the regulator, and the duty to do that rests with the business that is responsible, so know in advance who that would be.

General information for buyers, not legal advice. Check current requirements with the regulator or your own adviser.

## What needs reporting?

- **Hazards:** things that could cause harm, such as a leaking pipe, a damaged floor or a blocked exit.
- **Near misses:** something that could have injured someone but did not.
- **Incidents and injuries:** any injury, illness or damage involving a cleaner or anyone else on your site.
- **Security events:** unauthorised entry, lost keys or suspicious activity.
- **Property damage and complaints.**

## How should reporting work?

Name a person at your site and a person at the contractor who receive reports, with phone numbers. Set expectations on timing: urgent hazards straight away by phone, other matters by the next working day in writing. Use a simple log with date, location, description, action taken and who closed it out. Our [monthly report guide](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/what-a-monthly-cleaning-report-should-show/) covers how these appear in reporting.

## When does an incident have to be notified to the regulator?

In Queensland, certain serious events must be notified to WorkSafe Queensland, including deaths, serious injuries or illnesses, and dangerous incidents, and the business responsible must notify straight after becoming aware. WorkSafe explains [what is notifiable and how to notify](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/safety-and-prevention/incidents-and-notifications/notify-us-of-an-incident/notify-workplace-health-and-safety-queensland-or-electrical-safety-office). Because duties can overlap where a contractor works on your site, agree with the contractor in advance how each of you tells the other immediately and who notifies, and get advice if unsure.

## What should you do after an incident?

Make the area safe, look after the injured person, report as required, preserve the scene where appropriate, and record what happened. Then review the cause with the contractor and fix it. Keep the records with your [contractor documents](/resources/compliance-and-risk/records-and-reporting/documents-a-contractor-should-give-you/).

## What should the contract say?

Require the contractor to report hazards and incidents promptly, to cooperate with investigations and to keep its own records. See [cleaning contract terms to check](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/cleaning-contract-terms-to-check/).
