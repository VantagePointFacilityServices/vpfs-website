Before the first clean, collect the contract and scope, current insurance certificates, a list of the people assigned to your site with confirmation of their checks and induction, safety data sheets for the products used, and a statement on pay compliance. Then diarise renewal dates and ask for updates when anything changes.

General information for buyers, not legal advice. Check current requirements with the regulator or your own adviser.

## The contractor document checklist

| Document | Why you want it | Keep current |
|---|---|---|
| Signed contract and scope schedule | Defines what is delivered and what is excluded | On any change |
| Certificates of currency (public liability, workers' compensation) | Shows cover for the term | Each renewal |
| Business details: legal name, ABN, contact and supervisor | Confirms who you are contracting with | On change |
| Names of assigned staff and written confirmation of checks | Controls who enters your building | When staff change |
| Induction records | Shows safety information was passed on | Each new person |
| Safety data sheets and product list | Allows you to manage chemical risk | When products change |
| Site safety procedures or method statement | Shows how the work will be done safely | On scope change |
| Pay compliance statement | Evidence the contractor meets its obligations | Annually or on request |
| Key and access register | Controls entry to your building | On change |
| Inspection and monthly reports | Evidence of quality and issues | Monthly |

## Who should hold them?

One named person in your organisation should own the file, such as the facility manager or the body corporate manager. Keep both a digital folder and a short summary page with expiry dates. In a strata scheme, file them with the body corporate records.

## How do you keep them current?

Diarise insurance expiry dates and ask for the renewed certificate before the old one lapses. Review the whole file at each contract review, and again whenever the contractor changes people, products or scope.

## What if the contractor will not provide something?

Treat it as a finding, not a favour. Ask why, set a date and escalate if it is not met. Where it concerns insurance, safety or pay, do not let work continue indefinitely without it. See [warning signs your cleaner is underperforming](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/choosing-and-switching/warning-signs-your-cleaner-is-underperforming/).

## How does this fit the contract?

Write the delivery of these documents, and their renewal, into the contract. See [cleaning contract terms to check](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/cleaning-contract-terms-to-check/).
