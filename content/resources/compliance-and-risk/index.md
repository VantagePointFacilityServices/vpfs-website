When you hire a commercial cleaner you are not only buying a service, you are taking on a relationship with legal and safety consequences. The cleaner works in your building, uses chemicals, may work alone after hours, and is paid under rules that apply to their employer but can become your problem if they are broken. Managing that risk is mostly a matter of asking the right questions up front and keeping the right records.

General information for buyers, not legal advice. Check current requirements with the regulator or your own adviser.

## What risks do you take on?

Three areas matter most. The first is work health and safety: as the business running the workplace you have duties that continue when someone else's staff are working there. The second is whether the contractor is properly insured and complies with the rules on how cleaners are paid and engaged. The third is evidence: if something goes wrong, can you show what you checked and when?

## How does this fit the buying process?

Compliance checks belong early, in the same step as comparing quotes and choosing a contractor. A contractor who cannot show current insurance, a chemical register or a clear answer on how staff are paid should not reach the shortlist, however low the price.
