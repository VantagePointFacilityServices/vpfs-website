Ask every cleaning contractor to confirm in writing how its cleaners are engaged and that pay meets the Cleaning Services Award or the correct alternative, and treat a price that looks too low as a reason to ask more questions. Underpayment in cleaning is a known problem, and the businesses at the top of a cleaning supply chain can be exposed when it happens.

General information for buyers, not legal advice. Check current requirements with the regulator or your own adviser.

## Why should you care how another business pays its staff?

Wages are the biggest cost in cleaning, so the cheapest quote is often the one that cuts there. The Fair Work Ombudsman has run inquiries into cleaning supply chains and found serious non-compliance, and has urged businesses that engage cleaners to improve how they govern their contractors. See its [media release on the Woolworths cleaners inquiry](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/news-and-media-releases/2018-media-releases/february-2018/20180214-ww-cleaners). Beyond legal exposure there are practical risks: unhappy cleaners, high turnover and poor work.

## What does the Award cover?

The [Cleaning Services Award](https://awards.fairwork.gov.au/MA000022) sets minimum pay rates, loadings, penalty rates for evenings, weekends and public holidays, and minimum shift lengths for most commercial cleaners. Other arrangements can apply, so ask the contractor which instrument covers its people and have them confirm it.

## What should you ask?

- Are the cleaners employees or subcontractors, and how do you decide which?
- Which Award or agreement applies, and what classification and rates do you use?
- How are evening, weekend and public-holiday shifts paid?
- Do you keep time and wage records, and could you provide a declaration that staff on our site are paid correctly?
- How many layers of subcontracting are between us and the person who cleans our building?

## How do you test a low quote?

Ask the contractor how many labour hours sit behind the price and what hourly cost that implies. If the answer cannot cover the rules in the Award plus super and insurance, the quote is relying on something that will not hold. See [how commercial cleaning is priced](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/how-commercial-cleaning-is-priced/) and [how to compare cleaning quotes like for like](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/pricing-quotes-and-scope/compare-cleaning-quotes/).

## What should the contract say?

Require the contractor to comply with all applicable workplace laws, to give you evidence on request and to be responsible for its own subcontractors. See [cleaning contract terms to check](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/cleaning-contract-terms-to-check/).
