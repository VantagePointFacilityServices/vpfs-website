A cleaning contractor should be able to give you a current safety data sheet for every hazardous chemical it uses on your site, show a chemical register, explain how products are stored, labelled and used, and show that its staff are trained. If it cannot, it is not managing chemical risk, and your people and building are exposed.

General information for buyers, not legal advice. Check current requirements with the regulator or your own adviser.

## What is a safety data sheet?

A safety data sheet (SDS) is a document from the manufacturer or importer that describes a hazardous chemical, its hazards and how to use, store and handle it safely. WorkSafe Queensland explains the [labelling and safety data sheet requirements](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/safety-and-prevention/hazards/hazardous-chemicals/managing-hazchem-risks/labelling-and-safety-data-sheets), including that sheets should be current. Ask for the SDS for every product the contractor will use at your site, and check that they are the product versions in use.

## What else should the contractor have?

Business Queensland's guidance on [assessing the risks of hazardous chemicals](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/running-business/whs/hazardous-chemicals/assess-risks) describes the usual building blocks: identify the hazardous chemicals, keep a register with current safety data sheets and manage the risks. For a cleaning contractor on your site, that means:

- A list of products used on your site, with SDS attached.
- Clear labelling on containers, including any decanted into smaller bottles.
- Safe storage, away from children, food and incompatible products.
- Correct dilution, with measuring or dosing systems where possible.
- Training in safe use, including never mixing products.
- Personal protective equipment where the SDS requires it.
- Spill and exposure procedures.

## How does this connect to eco and disinfectant claims?

If the contractor says its products are eco-friendly or disinfectant-grade, the SDS and any certification are how you check. See [how to verify a cleaning contractor's eco claims](/resources/eco-friendly-cleaning/how-to-verify-eco-cleaning-claims/).

## What should you do with the documents?

Keep the SDS file with your contractor records and update it when products change. If your own business has a chemical register, ask the contractor to share changes so yours stays accurate.

## What are red flags?

No SDS on request, unlabelled bottles, products brought on site that are not on the list, staff who cannot say what a product is, and storage in unsafe places such as under sinks in kitchens used by children.
