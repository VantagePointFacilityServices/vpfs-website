Ask every cleaning contractor for current certificates of currency covering public liability and workers' compensation, and read them before you sign: check the insured entity's name, the cover type and the period. Insurance is the contractor's financial protection and yours if something goes wrong in your building, so a gap matters.

General information for buyers, not legal advice. Check current requirements with the regulator or your own adviser.

## What cover should a cleaning contractor hold?

- **Public liability:** covers injury to other people or damage to property caused by the contractor's work, for example someone slipping on a wet floor or damage to your equipment. Ask what amount of cover is held and what it excludes.
- **Workers' compensation:** covers the contractor's workers if they are injured at work. In Queensland, employers generally must hold this cover for their workers. Ask for the certificate from the insurer, which may be WorkCover Queensland or a self-insurer.
- **Other cover:** some contractors also hold products or professional indemnity cover, and some contracts ask for cover for loss of keys or property in care. Decide what suits your site.

## How do you check the certificates?

A certificate of currency should show the insured business name, the type of cover, the policy period and the insurer. Check that the name matches the contractor you are contracting with, not a related company, and that the period covers your whole contract. Diarise the expiry date and ask for the renewed certificate each year.

## What about subcontractors?

If your contractor uses subcontractors, ask how they are covered. Whether a person counts as a worker for workers' compensation purposes depends on the arrangement, not on how they are paid for tax. Ask the contractor to confirm in writing how every person on your site is covered and to name the insurer.

## What should the contract say?

Require the contractor to maintain the cover for the whole term, to provide renewed certificates on request, to notify you of any cancellation and to be responsible for its own staff and subcontractors. See [cleaning contract terms to check](/resources/hiring-a-cleaner/contracts-and-quality/cleaning-contract-terms-to-check/).

## What are red flags?

A contractor who delays supplying certificates, who offers an expired one, whose insured name differs from the contracting name, or who says cover is "not needed" for small jobs.
