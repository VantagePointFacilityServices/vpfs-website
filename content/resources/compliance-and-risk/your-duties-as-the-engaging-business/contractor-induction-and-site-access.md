A contractor induction tells the cleaning team what they need to know about your site before they work in it: the hazards, the rules, the emergency arrangements and who to call. Combine it with controlled access, so only inducted, authorised people can get in, and keep a record of both.

General information for buyers, not legal advice. Check current requirements with the regulator or your own adviser.

## Why induct cleaners at all?

Cleaners arrive in a building they may not know, often after hours and sometimes alone. The induction is how you pass on what you know about the site, and how the contractor confirms their people understand it. It also forms part of your evidence that you took reasonable steps to manage shared risks. See [WHS duties when you hire a cleaning contractor](/resources/compliance-and-risk/your-duties-as-the-engaging-business/whs-duties-when-you-hire-a-cleaning-contractor/).

## What should an induction cover?

- **Emergency arrangements:** exits, assembly points, alarms, first-aid equipment and who to call.
- **Site hazards:** wet areas, machinery, restricted or hazardous areas, work at height and anything specific to your business.
- **Site rules:** areas that are off limits, security procedures, parking and any personal protective equipment required.
- **Chemicals and equipment:** where products are stored, what may be used and what must not be mixed.
- **Reporting:** how to report hazards, damage and incidents, and to whom.
- **Confidentiality:** what to do around sensitive information, clear desks and screens.

## How do you control access?

Keep a register of keys, fobs and codes, issued to named people. Issue individual codes where you can, so entry is traceable. Collect keys and cancel access when a worker leaves the contract or the contract ends. See [after-hours access and security](/resources/by-building-type/offices/after-hours-access-and-security/) for the detail.

## Who should be inducted?

Everyone who will work on your site, including relief and replacement cleaners. Make it a condition of the contract that new staff are inducted before their first shift, either by you or by the contractor's supervisor using your written site information.

## What records should you keep?

Keep a signed list of who was inducted, when, by whom and what was covered, plus a copy of the material used. Review and repeat induction when the site, the scope or the hazards change.
