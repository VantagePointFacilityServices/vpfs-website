When you engage a cleaning contractor, work health and safety duties are shared, not transferred. Both your business and the contractor are usually persons conducting a business or undertaking, and where duties overlap each must consult, cooperate and coordinate with the other so the risks at your site are managed together. A contract does not remove your duty.

General information for buyers, not legal advice. Check current requirements with the regulator or your own adviser.

## Who is responsible for what?

You are generally responsible for the workplace you control: the building, its access, its hazards and its emergency arrangements. The contractor is responsible for its own workers, its methods, its equipment and its chemicals. Where the two meet, for example a cleaner working in a plant room or using a chemical near your staff, both have a role. Queensland's [code of practice on consultation, cooperation and coordination](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/laws-and-compliance/codes-of-practice/work-health-and-safety-consultation-cooperation-and-coordination-code-of-practice-2021-changes) explains how duty holders who share a workplace should work together.

## What should you do before the contractor starts?

- Tell the contractor about the hazards at your site that they could encounter: wet or slippery areas, machinery, restricted rooms, chemicals, asbestos registers or work at height.
- Find out about the hazards they bring: the chemicals they use, their equipment and their work methods.
- Agree who does what for emergencies, such as evacuation, first aid and who to call.
- Agree how problems are reported, in both directions.

## What does coordination look like during the contract?

Share changes that affect safety, such as building work, new equipment or altered access, with the contractor. Ask them to tell you about incidents, near misses and hazards they find. Review the arrangement when the scope changes. Write all of this into the contract or a site safety schedule.

## What evidence should you keep?

Keep the contractor's insurance certificates, their safety documents for your site, records of induction and a note of any hazards or incidents reported and what was done. These are what you will rely on if you are ever asked to show what you did.

## How does this vary by site?

Risk profiles differ. A clinic raises infection-control questions, a school raises children's safety, and a warehouse raises vehicle and machinery hazards.
