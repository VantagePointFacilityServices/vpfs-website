Who this is for

Practice and centre managers who want the general areas cleaned to an agreed scope

If you manage a medical centre or GP clinic on the Gold Coast, you shouldn’t have to guess what the cleaners are doing in the areas your patients and staff use every day. We agree the scope in writing, clean to it, and measure the result.

What we quote for: the general areas of your centre — reception, waiting rooms, offices, staff rooms and bathrooms.

What we don’t quote for yet: treatment and procedure rooms are scoped once our clinical cleaning protocol is in place. Medical enquiries are quoted for general areas only.

What’s in the written scope

A general-area scope you can read and hold us to

This is the clean our crew works to in the general areas of a medical centre. Your written scope sets out which of these apply to your rooms, and how often. Treatment and procedure rooms are not part of it.

  • Reception: counters, desks, pens and shared equipment wiped, glass and floors cleaned
  • Waiting rooms: seating and side tables wiped, magazines and toys tidied, floors cleaned
  • Offices: desks, shelves and shared equipment dusted high to low
  • Staff rooms: counters, sinks, appliance exteriors, tables and chairs, with dishes handled or loaded into the dishwasher
  • Bathrooms: toilets, urinals, sinks and mirrors cleaned fixture by fixture in a set order, with the correct colour-coded cloth for each
  • Consumables restocked (toilet paper, paper towels, hand soap) during the same bathroom pass
  • Touchpoints wiped on every visit: door handles, light switches, counters, shared equipment
  • Bins emptied and floors done last

The boundary, plainly: treatment and procedure rooms are scoped once our clinical cleaning protocol is in place. Until then, we quote medical centres for the general areas above.

How quality is checked

Inspected by someone who didn’t do the clean

A cleaner marking their own work isn’t a check. After the clean, a supervisor who wasn’t on the roster inspects the site and scores it against your written scope.

You receive a monthly cleaning report covering the agreed scope, so you see the result without having to ask.

Method & products

How the crew works through your general areas

Order of operations

Top to bottom, clean to dirty, furthest point to the exit, floors last, so nothing is re-soiled after it has been cleaned.

Colour-coded microfibre

Red for toilets and urinals, yellow for restroom surfaces, sinks and counters, blue for general surfaces and glass, green for kitchens and breakrooms. A cloth never crosses categories, and cloths are laundered separately by colour.

Measured dilution

Chemicals are diluted to the ratio on the product label, not eyeballed. Diluted bottles are labelled with the product name and ratio, and chemicals are never mixed across categories.

Why clean general areas matter

For your staff, and for the people who visit

For staff. In a 2019 study of an office building, a hygiene programme (disinfectant spray on high-touch surfaces plus hand sanitiser and wipes for staff) reduced the amount of a tracer virus on workers’ hands and on surfaces by an average of 85.4%.1 That study measured tracer-virus contamination, not illness, so we don’t claim it as a health outcome. It was done in an office, not a clinic, but it does show why we wipe a fixed list of touchpoints on every visit.

For your obligations. The Queensland Managing the work environment and facilities Code of Practice 2021 gives practical guidance on the physical work environment and on facilities for workers, such as toilets, drinking water and washing areas.2 A written scope and a monthly report give you a record of what was done.

For customers. Visitors form a view of your business from the first room they see. A reception, waiting room and bathroom that are consistently clean tell them the place is looked after.

  1. Kurgat EK et al. (2019), “Impact of a hygiene intervention on virus spread in an office building”, International Journal of Hygiene and Environmental Health 222(3):479–485. Read the abstract on PubMed.
  2. Queensland Government, Managing the work environment and facilities Code of Practice 2021. Read the code (PDF, WorkSafe Queensland).
